## 1. Purpose

This document identifies the stakeholders involved in the proposed laboratory data governance and interoperability programme.

It outlines their responsibilities, expectations, influence on the project and proposed communication approach.

The stakeholder roles are based on the hypothetical multi-branch hospital scenario used in this portfolio project.

## 2. Stakeholder Register

| Stakeholder | Primary interest | Expected contribution |
|---|---|---|
| Hospital Executive Sponsor | Business value, cost and strategic alignment | Project sponsorship and major decisions |
| Laboratory Director | Laboratory data accuracy and consistency | Clinical and operational requirements |
| Clinical Informatics Lead | Standardised clinical terminology | LOINC governance and clinical validation |
| IT / HIS Manager | System compatibility and integration | Technical requirements and architecture |
| Laboratory Technologists | Accurate recording and reporting | Workflow feedback and practical validation |
| Quality Manager | Data quality and auditability | Quality indicators and monitoring requirements |
| Data Analyst | Reliable and usable data | Dataset profiling and analysis |
| Treating Clinicians | Reliable laboratory results | Clinical usability requirements |
| Patients | Accurate and accessible reports | Indirect beneficiaries of improvements |

## 3. Power–Interest Analysis

### High Power / High Interest — Manage Closely

**Laboratory Director**
- Responsible for laboratory operations.
- Provides key requirements for laboratory data quality.
- Reviews proposed terminology and unit standardisation rules.

**IT / HIS Manager**
- Responsible for technical feasibility.
- Reviews interoperability and system integration requirements.
- Advises on technical constraints.

### High Power / Low Interest — Keep Satisfied

**Hospital Executive Sponsor**
- Interested in strategic outcomes and business value.
- Requires periodic progress reports.
- Approves major project decisions and resources.

### Low Power / High Interest — Keep Informed

**Laboratory Technologists**
- Provide practical feedback on laboratory workflows.
- Help identify common data entry inconsistencies.
- Participate in validation and acceptance activities.

**Treating Clinicians**
- Provide feedback on the usability and interpretation of laboratory information.
- Review clinical implications of standardisation decisions.

**Data Analyst**
- Supports dataset assessment and reporting.
- Communicates analytical findings.

### Low Power / Low Interest — Monitor

Other indirectly affected administrative or support teams may be monitored for relevant changes in project requirements.

*Note: These classifications are initial assumptions for the case study. They should be validated through stakeholder discussions in a real implementation.*

## 5. Key Stakeholder Questions

The following questions should be discussed during stakeholder interviews.

### Hospital Management
1. What business outcomes are expected from improving laboratory data quality?
2. Which performance indicators should management monitor?
3. What are the major operational concerns?

### Laboratory Director
1. Which laboratory tests are most frequently affected by inconsistent naming?
2. Are different units used for the same test?
3. How are reference ranges currently maintained?
4. Who approves laboratory terminology changes?

### IT / HIS Manager
1. Which systems currently store laboratory observations?
2. What formats are used for data exchange?
3. Are standard healthcare interoperability interfaces available?
4. What technical constraints could affect implementation?

### Clinical Informatics Lead
1. Which terminology standards are currently used?
2. How should uncertain terminology mappings be reviewed?
3. How should clinical meaning be preserved during standardisation?

### Quality Manager
1. Which data quality indicators are currently monitored?
2. How are data errors identified and corrected?
3. Who is responsible for closing data quality exceptions?

### Laboratory Technologists and Clinicians
1. What common data entry problems occur?
2. What makes laboratory results difficult to interpret?
3. What improvements would make daily workflows easier?

## 6. Key Stakeholder Risks

| Risk | Potential impact | Proposed response |
|---|---|---|
| Conflicting stakeholder priorities | Delays in requirements approval | Agree on priorities and escalation routes |
| Limited clinical engagement | Incorrect assumptions about clinical workflows | Include clinical representatives in reviews |
| Lack of technical input | Unrealistic integration requirements | Involve IT early |
| Unclear decision ownership | Delayed approvals | Define decision-making responsibilities |
| Resistance to workflow changes | Poor adoption | Involve end users and provide training |

## 7. Decision-Making and Governance

The following proposed responsibilities will guide project decisions:

- Executive Sponsor: Strategic approval and major scope decisions.
- Laboratory Director: Laboratory workflow and operational requirements.
- Clinical Informatics Lead: Clinical terminology and semantic validation.
- IT / HIS Manager: Technical feasibility and integration review.
- Quality Manager: Data quality indicators and validation expectations.
- Project Manager / Business Analyst: Coordination, documentation, requirements traceability and milestone reporting.

## 8. Conclusion

Effective stakeholder engagement is essential for successful healthcare technology implementation.

The project will use structured communication, early stakeholder involvement and clearly defined decision-making responsibilities to ensure that clinical, operational and technical requirements are appropriately represented.
